Learning notes, model limitations & sources

Explore in three steps

Hover over the anatomy to see its name. Hover over the SA or AV node to reveal a cellular action potential; move away to hide it. Keyboard focus reveals the same details, and Escape dismisses them. The cell illustration follows the study speed and pauses with playback.

Study speed: 1× = 1/10 real time; 1.25× = 1/8; 2.5× = 1/4 (fastest).

1. Start in sinus rhythm at 1× study speed. Follow the yellow wave across the heart.

2. Pause or drag the time slider. The vertical line marks the exact instant shown on the heart and all twelve traces.

3. Select II, aVR, then V1 and V6. Compare the sign and shape of the same event from different directions. Then change the rhythm.

Electrodes ≠ leads

A standard ECG uses 10 electrodes to create 12 leads. I = LA − RA; II = LL − RA; III = LL − LA. aVR, aVL and aVF compare one limb with the average of the other two. V1–V6 compare a chest electrode with the Wilson central terminal (average of RA, LA and LL). RL is the reference/ground electrode and does not form a diagnostic lead.

What this model can and cannot show

The plumbing view links illustrative valve timing and ventricular motion to the simulated ECG. The ultrasound-style view is a schematic apical four-chamber illustration, not real ultrasound or Doppler. Mechanical animation supports sinus rhythm, bradycardia, tachycardia and first-degree AV block; other patterns are explicitly unmodeled.

This is an original, simplified cross-section conduction diagram with synthetic example ECGs, not a patient-specific anatomical reconstruction or a diagnostic simulator. Chamber shapes, wavefronts and lead axes are schematic. Chest markers indicate relative viewing directions; see each lead’s description for clinical placement. Limb markers are axes, not literal electrode locations.

The frontal cutaway shows the atria, AV junction and branching ventricular pathways. The transverse view shows a ventricular slice; the small SA → AV → His route above it is an upstream timing reference outside that slice. Only the matching lead family is shown. Lead and key electrical labels stay outside the heart. Chamber labels are optional. Hover or tap a labeled electrical structure for its location.

Yellow dots and trails follow connected paths from the SA node through atrial muscle, the AV delay, His bundle, bundle branches and Purkinje network into myocardial branches. Sub-path timing is schematic and compressed into the ECG intervals; early septal activation precedes the terminal ventricular branches. Atrial paths illustrate spread through muscle, not necessarily discrete anatomical tracts. The AV and His signals themselves are too small to form separate surface ECG waves.

Purple illumination represents recovery of myocardial branches during the T wave. It is not a new impulse and does not travel backward along the conduction tree. The direction and timing of recovery are simplified. Abnormal rhythms may originate outside the SA node; blocked bundles use an explicitly separate route through myocardium. VF has no single coherent impulse.

The arrow shows a simplified net electrical vector, not a traveling particle. Limb axes lie in the frontal plane; chest leads sample the horizontal plane. These are viewing directions, not isolated sources. Chest waveforms and regional ST changes remain illustrative approximations, not a solved cardiac volume-conductor model.
